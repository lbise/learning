Shapes and materials

Grant Abbitt's Parts 1–2, watched with Blender open, connected to what you already know from Godot. Then make two props for your own game.

Goal
Navigate Blender, place and transform objects, and give them materials that will survive the trip to Godot.
Time
About 25 min of video, which takes 60–75 min when you follow along, plus about 30 min for the props. Splitting it over two sessions is fine.

① Set up (5 minutes)

  1. Launch Blender (you have 5.2.2 LTS; the course uses 5.0, so expect tiny UI differences). Arrange it next to the video, or on a second screen.
  2. No numpad on your keyboard? Go to Edit → Preferences → Input → Emulate Numpad. The manual: “The Numpad keys are used quite often in Blender and are not assigned to the same action as the regular number keys.”
  3. Make a folder ~/learning/gamedev/blender/ for your .blend files.

② Watch: and do every step yourself

Same principle as Lessons 1–3: watching feels like learning, but doing it is what makes it stick. If you catch yourself just watching, rewind 30 seconds and do it.

Pause and copy every action. Grant moves fast and the shortcuts only stick when your own hands do them. Below, the chapter list from each video's description, with what to look out for.

④ On your own: props for your game

Use only what Parts 1–2 taught: adding shapes, the Adjust Last Operation panel, G/R/S with axes, and Principled BSDF materials. The sizes match your Godot game, which uses metres in both programs.

The coin

  1. New file (General). Delete the default cube.
  2. Add a Cylinder. In Adjust Last Operation set Radius 0.4 m, Depth 0.1 m, and Vertices 24, which gives a lower-poly look. Same size as the Godot coin from Lesson 3.
  3. Stand it upright: rotate it 90° around X.
  4. Give it a new material named Gold: a gold Base Color, Metallic 1.0, Roughness ≈ 0.3. Check it in Material Preview.
  5. Name the object Coin (double-click it in the Outliner).

The tree

  1. Move the coin aside (say 3 m along X) so you have room.
  2. Trunk: a Cylinder with radius 0.15 m, depth 1.5 m and 8 vertices. Move it up so its bottom sits on the ground (Z = 0). Where must its centre be?
  3. Foliage: a Cone with 8 vertices, Radius 1 of 0.9 m, Radius 2 of 0 and depth 2 m. Place it so it overlaps the top of the trunk a little.
  4. Materials: Bark (brown, Roughness 0.9) on the trunk and Leaves (green, Roughness 0.8) on the cone. Name the objects Trunk and Foliage.
  5. Check it from the front view in orthographic (Numpad1, Numpad5): is the trunk's bottom exactly on the ground line?
  6. Save as ~/learning/gamedev/blender/props.blend.

Hint: where the trunk's centre goes

Show hintA new cylinder's origin is at its centre, so half of it sits below the ground. Depth 1.5 → move it up by half: G Z 0.75 Enter. The cone (depth 2) then needs its centre at about 1.5 + 1 − a little overlap ≈ 2.3 m. Same reasoning as the crate in Lesson 2, where you set the mesh to y = 0.5.

Stretch: make it feel more “made” (only if you have time)

Show ideasRotate the cone a few degrees around Z so its facets don't line up with the trunk's. Add a second, smaller cone above the first for a fuller pine. Make a variant: a short, wide tree with a sphere (UV Sphere, low segment count) for foliage. Variety matters more than detail in a level.

Keep the .blend. Lesson 9 covers origins, joining the tree into one object, applying transforms, and exporting to .glb. Then your coin replaces the cylinder in your Godot game, and trees go into the level.
